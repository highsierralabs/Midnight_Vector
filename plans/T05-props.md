## 1. Purpose

This task is where "full of stuff" lives (R3). After T05, a viewer flying a fake world through `dev/props.html` sees a road lined with palms, reflector posts and billboards, a skyline of stepped towers, pyramids and needles, saucers and chrome orbs overhead, and landmark rings that frame the sun. Everything glows in neon, reflects in the floor and streams past forever. Observable outcomes:

1. **Every object is recognisable.** A contact sheet shows all 20 archetypes, 6 items and the ship, up close and at their true on-screen size at range, plus a black-on-white silhouette sheet with no labels. The executor has opened those images and can name every tile. No two silhouettes overlap by more than the gate in section 7.
2. **Nothing ever pops, at any distance.** Walking forward through 300 chunk crossings produces the same pixels as warping straight to that spot, including across the 65536 row wrap and near `CHUNK_LIMIT`.
3. **It costs what §9 allows.** At worst the layer issues 42 draw calls and about 530k triangles, one `fillProps` per chunk crossing, and zero allocations in its pure hot path.
4. **It survives everything the app can do to it:** warp, context restore, tier changes with nothing vanishing in one frame (a near tower does collapse in about 36 ms, accepted in DI-42), and rings going idle for any length of time.
5. **The neon holds up in motion.** Far edges fade to their coverage rather than sparkling, and a filtered far field measures lower shimmer than a naive one.

Terms:

- **Record:** one 8-float entry in a `PropRow`, or one in an `ItemRow`.
- **Ring:** one `InstancedBufferGeometry` per archetype or item kind, plus a fixed-capacity CPU copy of its instance data. New records overwrite the oldest slots in strict FIFO order, except in the three rings with an additive draw (SAUCER, SEARCHLIGHT, FENCE), which slide so their slot order equals a rebuild's (§6.5 as amended by DI-62).
- **Slot:** one instance position in a ring.
- **Reach `R`:** `ceil(maxDist / 64)`, how many rows ahead of the camera chunk a ring must already hold.
- **Live:** a record whose forward distance `zf` is in `[-30, maxDist]`, the test the vertex shader applies.
- **Crossing:** `v.cam.chunk` increased by exactly 1 since the last frame.
- **Modulus:** the slot count a ring currently wraps at; equal to capacity at full density, lower at reduced density.
- **Filter:** the density at which a ring accepts records (`keep' < filter`).
- **Ring keep (`keep'`):** the value a ring stores, tests and sends to the shader: `max(keep, rank / (perChunk * 1.25))`, where `rank` is the record's 0-based position in its row and archetype by ascending `keep`, ties by ordinal (DI-41). T01's record keeps its own `keep`.
- **FIFO equals rebuild:** walking to a state through crossings must render the same as a `rebuild` at that state.
- **Tile, sheet:** one image cell, and the page of cells composed in a 2D canvas.
- **Backdrop:** a dev-only stand-in for T03 and T04, made only of contract GLSL, so the mirror and silhouettes have something to sit against.

**The live-row model, which everything here rests on.** With camera at `(c, local)` and a record in row `r` at `zLocal`, `zf = (r - c) * 64 + zLocal - local`. A brute-force sweep over all `local` and `zLocal` on a 0.25 u lattice gave live rows `c - 1 .. c + ceil(maxDist / 64)` for all seven reaches [O: `arith.mjs` in the session scratchpad]. So capacity `perChunk * (R + 2)` holds every live record, and equals the §6.5 formula `perChunk * ceil(maxDist / 64 + 2)` for every roster entry except RING [O: 0 mismatches against the Cap column as it stood then], and TRAFFIC's moving cars need one rear row more, capacity 116 (§6.5). It is exactly enough only if a crossing feeds the new row **after** the camera has moved, because row `c - 1` is then dead (`zf <= -64`) when it gets overwritten. Distinct reaches: props 13, 19, 25, 32, 47, 63, 94; items 13, 25 [O].

**Where the evidence comes from.** Every `[O]` here comes from a throwaway spike run on 2026-11-05 in the session scratchpad, which is not part of the project and may be gone. It used three 0.187.0 on the RTX 4070 SUPER with a direct-to-canvas depth buffer, MSAA 0 and no composer. All 26 rings had worst-case record counts, but every ring drew the same pyramid. None of that counts as acceptance, and none of the art has been built. The executor runs everything again. Section 10 has the numbers.

### M1. Ring and feeder, pure, in Node

**Goal:** the FIFO and the row cache are provably correct with no GPU. **Work:**

`plan.ts`. For each entry: `id`, `source` (`prop` or `item`), `kind`, `maxDist`, `perChunk`, `reach = ceil(maxDist / 64)`, `rear`, `cap`. `rear = 1` except TRAFFIC, where `rear = ceil((30 + TRAFFIC_TRAVEL_MAX) / 64) = 3`. `cap = perChunk * (reach + 1 + rear)` except RING, which is 4. The result:

`270, 390, 130, 63, 162, 81, 98, 480, 272, 98, 49, 126, 49, 4, 147, 34, 68, 34, 168, 60 | 180, 27, 60, 30, 45, 116`

That matches §6.5's Cap column. RING's 4 holds with `periodChunks = 1024`, where a wrap can bring two records 16 chunks apart, because rows `c - 1 .. c + 94` then hold at most 4 records [arithmetic].

`ring.ts`, class `RingStore`:

- `arr: Float32Array(cap * 12)` in the instance layout below. `head`, `modulus`, and a circular `rowCount` of `reach + 1 + rear` entries, whose sum is `liveRecords`.
- `writeRow(chunk, src, start, end, filter, perRowClamp)`: copies accepted records one at a time with a plain loop, wrapping at `modulus` **per record**, so a row can split across the wrap and no fragmentation can occur. Reports one or two ranges through a caller-owned scratch.
- **Ring keep (DI-41).** On prop rings only, since an item has no `keep` and stores 0 per the layout below: before copying, `writeRow` ranks the slice by ascending `keep`, ties by ordinal, with an insertion sort on preallocated scratch sized by `MAX_PROPS_PER_CHUNK`, so that an over-full row still reaches the `truncated` count, and computes `keep' = max(keep, rank / (perChunk * 1.25))`. A record is accepted when `Math.fround(keep') < Math.fround(filter)`, the same comparison the shader makes, and the slot stores `keep'`. `keep'` depends only on the row, so FIFO and rebuild write equal values, and a refill writes the values the old slots held. A record that passes has `rank < perChunk * d * 1.25`, so for any `d` at most `ceil(perChunk * d * 1.25)` pass, and the clamp never cuts a live record. Passing sets are nested as `d` rises. RING stays 0 and PYLON stays `.1` and `.6`. A row of at most `perChunk` records lifts no value above `(perChunk - 1) / (perChunk * 1.25)`, which is under .8 [O: arithmetic only, 5,720,000 row-density cases for each of five keep models, 0 over the clamp, 0 nesting breaks, in a throwaway script. Not yet run in a ring, a shader or a frame; A2d and A12 do that].
- A new ring, and any ring after `reset()`, has every slot dead: `keep = 2`, above any density. `reset()` sets `head = 0`, so a rebuild from equal rows gives the same slot order (§8.1).
- **Idle transition.** When `liveRecords` falls to 0, mark every slot dead once. Otherwise a slot untouched for 65536 chunks could alias back to live through `rowDelta`.
- At most `perRowClamp(filter)` records accepted per row. Extras increment `truncated`. With `keep'`, that happens only for a row holding more than `perChunk`, which T01 rules out, so `truncated` stays 0 (A9).

`feeder.ts`:

- `propRows`: 96 `PropRow`s, addressed by `chunk % 96`, each with a `.loaded` chunk. They hold rows `c - 1 .. c + 94` exactly.
- `itemRows`: 32 `ItemRow`s, addressed by `chunk % 32`, holding rows `c - 3 .. c + 25`, each with `slot: Int16Array(MAX_ITEMS_PER_CHUNK)` mapping record ordinal to ring slot, -1 if unfed.
- `cross(c)`: `fillProps(c + 94)`, `fillItems(c + 25)`, then for each ring `r = c + reach`, write the slice from the cached row. Items scan the row by kind, since an `ItemRow` has no `start` table.
- `rebuild(c)`: fill all rows, `reset()` all rings, write rows ascending from `c - rear` to `c + reach`, skipping `r < 0`. Identical inputs give identical buffers, regardless of history.
- `hideItem(chunk, index)`: find the slot, write `keep = 2`, report one range of one float. Ignore unknown rows and unfed records.

**Instance layout**, 12 floats per slot. Since R3 (D-006) one `InstancedBufferAttribute` of itemSize 12 per ring, shared by the ring's main, mirror and beam geometry and read in the shader as `in mat4 aInst`, four 3-float columns, which the shader reassembles into the three logical attributes below by swizzle (until R3, one `InstancedInterleavedBuffer` with three `InterleavedBufferAttribute`s, which uploaded the same one GL buffer but which section 9.3's scene audit counted three to six times):

| Attribute | Props | Items |
|---|---|---|
| `aInstA` | `x, y, zLocal, row` | `x, y, zLocal, row` |
| `aInstB` | `yaw, scale, keep', tintSeed` | `TAU * seed, radius or 1, 0 (2 when hidden), seed` |
| `aInstC` | `variant, 0, 0, 0` | `seed, a, b, radius` |

`row` is `rowMod(chunk)`. SHARD's second attribute value is 1, not its `radius`, per the seam. 3241 instances at 48 B is 152 KB, under §9.3's 0.2 MB, counted once per ring by the audit since R3 (A9b).

**Tests** are in section 7. The core is a **model check**: after each crossing, at 8 values of `local`, the set of live records computed from ring slots must equal the set computed from `fillProps` over all rows, with no missing record, no extra and no duplicate. It runs against `worstCaseWorld.ts`, which emits exactly `perChunk` everywhere, with an independent `hash01` `keep` per record like T00's fake (the harder case for the clamp) except RING and PYLON, which copy T01's values, at bases 0, 65400, `2^40 - 100` and `2^48 - 2024`, and includes the TRAFFIC forward offset at sampled phases. **The plan is a parameter, so negative controls pass a mutated plan** rather than adding test hooks to production code. **Result:** ring logic trusted. **Proof:** A1 to A3.

The vertex path, in `props.vert.glsl`, after `COMMON_VERT`:

```glsl
in vec4 aEdge; in vec2 aUv; in vec2 aTag;       // per vertex: edge distances (u), panel uv (u), part id and param
in vec4 aInstA, aInstB, aInstC;
uniform float uMaxDist, uDensity, uGhost, uTrafficFade;   // uTrafficFade is TRAFFIC_FADE, so T02 and T05 share one number
uniform int uMotion;
out vec4 vEdge; out vec2 vUv; out vec3 vRel; flat out vec4 vI; out float vPart;

void main() {
  float fwd = 0., sMul = 1.;
  if (uMotion == M_TRAFFIC) {
    float f = fract(aInstC.z * phaseAt(7) + aInstB.w);
    fwd = trafficOffset(aInstC.y, aInstC.z, aInstB.w, phaseAt(7));
    sMul = min(1., min(f, 1. - f) / uTrafficFade);
  }
  float zf = rowDelta(aInstA.w) * 64. + aInstA.z + fwd - uTravel.x;
  float md = min(uMaxDist, uFog.y);                       // nothing draws fully fogged, see the Decision log
  if (zf < -30. || zf > md || aInstB.z >= uDensity) { gl_Position = vec4(2., 2., 2., 1.); return; }
  float s = aInstB.y * sMul * (1. - smoothstep(md * .92, md, zf));
  s *= min((uDensity - aInstB.z) / .03, 1.);              // shrink as the density ramp passes, instead of a pop
  // ... motion by uMotion on the mesh-space position, then yaw, then scale ...
  vec3 p = vec3(aInstA.x, aInstA.y, -zf) + l;
#ifdef MIRROR
  if (abs(aInstA.x) > 125.) { gl_Position = vec4(2., 2., 2., 1.); return; }
  p.y = -max(p.y, 0.);                                    // clamp first: a buried root must not flip above the floor
#endif
  p = bendV(p);                                           // flip before bend, so the reflection plane bends with the floor
  vRel = p - vec3(cameraPosition.xy, 0.);
  gl_Position = projectionMatrix * viewMatrix * vec4(p, 1.);
}
```

Details the layer must get right:

- `frustumCulled = false` on every mesh. `computeBoundingSphere()` once in `attach`, since the renderer otherwise does it on first draw [O: `WebGLRenderer.js:1941`].
- Add every mesh in `attach`, under one root `Group` that `setVisible` toggles.
- `uDensity` uploads as `1.03` at full density, so the shrink factor is 1 for all `keep' < 1`.
- A ring's `visible` is `liveRecords > 0`. The mirror mesh also needs `q.mirror`.
- `frame(v)`: if `v.cam.chunk === last`, no streaming. If `last + 1`, `feeder.cross`. Anything else (backwards, or more than one chunk) goes through the same path as `rebuild` and increments `catchUps`.
- After each crossing, for each touched ring: `addUpdateRange` per reported range, `needsUpdate = true`. **A hidden mesh never uploads, so its ranges accumulate** [O: 30 pending after 30 crossings, cleared at first show]. If `updateRanges.length > 64`, call `clearUpdateRanges()`. The next upload is then a full `bufferSubData` [O: `WebGLAttributes.js`], which is the correct recovery.
- `rebuild(v)`: `feeder.rebuild`, then `clearUpdateRanges()` and `needsUpdate = true` on every ring. The same path serves warp and context restore.
- `trafficOffset` and `droneOffset` come from `common.glsl` (§8.2). T05 declares no function with either name.
- T05 never writes the shared uniforms. `uTravel`, `uCamRow`, `uPhases`, `uBend`, `uPal` and `uFog` arrive from `writeSharedUniforms` (T00), by reference.

**Density, in `density.ts`.** `q.propDensity` changes with `setQuality`. Each ring has a filter and modulus: `perRowClamp(d) = min(perChunk, ceil(perChunk * d * 1.25))`, `modulus(d) = perRowClamp(d) * (reach + 1 + rear)`. RING and all items stay at their full `cap` and never filter; T01 gives RING `keep = 0`.

- **Going down:** ramp `uDensity` from the old value to the new one over 90 ticks, as a pure function of `v.tick`. If `v.tick` goes backwards, finish immediately. Then **refill one ring per frame** from the row cache with the new filter and modulus, with a full upload.
- **Going up:** refill first, one ring per frame. The new records have `keep' >= uDensity`, so they stay collapsed. Then ramp up.
- **Why this works:** `keep'` keeps every row within its clamp at every density (DI-41), so every record still showing at the end of a ramp is accepted by the new filter. A refill changes only which dead slots exist, so it must be invisible, and a test checks exactly that.
- **First call and rebuild:** the first `setQuality` and every `rebuild` set `uDensity`, filter and modulus to the current target at once, with phase idle.
- **Request mid-change:** a new request during a ramp or refill restarts toward the new target from the current `uDensity`, with a refill of every ring whose filter differs from the new target. Modulus is not the test: every `perChunk` 1 ring keeps one modulus at all densities, so a ring refilled at a low filter and skipped on the way back up would miss records.

**Result:** a moving field of coloured boxes with correct retirement. **Proof:** A4 to A12.

### M3. Builders, neon material, mirror, and the two reference archetypes

**Goal:** the look, proven on two archetypes the prototype has already rendered: PYRAMID and PALM. **Work:**

`builders.ts`, full version:

```ts
export type V3 = readonly [number, number, number];
export interface FaceOpts { edges?: number; part?: number; param?: number; root?: boolean }   // edges: bitmask over the face's edges, default all
export class MeshBuilder {
  tri(a: V3, b: V3, c: V3, o?: FaceOpts): void;            // counter-clockwise seen from outside
  panel(a: V3, b: V3, c: V3, d: V3, o?: FaceOpts): void;   // planar quad, counter-clockwise seen from outside
  finish(): BuiltMesh;   // position, edge (vec4), uv (vec2, in u), tag (vec2), index (Uint16), tris, bbox
}
```

`aEdge` holds, per vertex, the distance in u to up to four edge lines. Distance to a line is affine on a plane, so it interpolates exactly over any planar quad or triangle. Edges not in the mask, and all edges on a `root` face, get the sentinel 1000. **Stock geometries are banned** (§6.5), and a unit test greps for them.

`shapes.ts`: the helpers in section 3, all through `panel` and `tri`. Each takes an edge policy, so a smooth shape can draw ring lines and leave its other seams dark.

`neon.glsl`, the filter, using increasing `smoothstep` edges and `min(max())` per `Agents.md`:

```glsl
float edgeLines(vec4 e, float lw) {            // e already multiplied by instance scale, so lw is a world width
  vec4 fw = max(fwidth(e), vec4(1e-5));
  vec4 draw = max(vec4(lw), fw), aa = fw * 1.5;
  vec4 l = (1. - smoothstep(draw - aa, draw + aa, e)) * clamp(vec4(lw) / draw, 0., 1.);
  return max(max(l.x, l.y), max(l.z, l.w));
}
```

Lines stay per edge rather than taking `min(e)` first, because `min` has a derivative discontinuity along the diagonals. This form rendered with no error [O].

`props.frag.glsl` is one program selected by uniforms (`uLook`, plus `uBodyL`, `uEdgeL`, `uEdgeW`) and a part id. Uniform branches are constant across a primitive, so derivatives inside them stay defined. Compute derivatives before branching regardless. Base recipe, all emission through `emit`, §6.7 for the values:

```
d = length(vRel); rd = vRel / d
tint = palette slot chosen by tintSeed from the archetype's two or three slots (uPal[2], [3], [5], [6])
c = uPal[0] * uBodyL  (body at most .2)
c += emit(tint, uEdgeL * (1 - .7 * fogK(d * 1.4))) * edgeLines(vEdge, uEdgeW)
look-specific term
MIRROR: c *= .42
c = mix(c, skyGrad(rd.y), fogK(d)); gl_FragColor = vec4(c, 1)
```

**At most 6 programs:** normal, mirror with `BackSide`, additive, `CUTOUT` (the only one containing `discard`, used by CASSETTE alone, because a `discard` in a shared program can cost every draw its early depth test [M]), and ship body and ship glow, which use `ship.vert.glsl`. `ShaderMaterial` with `glslVersion` unset, `in`/`out`, `gl_FragColor`, shared uniforms spread by reference, never `clone()`. Every other per-ring value is a uniform, not a define, so rings share programs and nothing recompiles when a tier changes.

**Filtering rule for every pattern a look draws** (windows, glyphs, stripes, spokes): take `fwidth` of the pattern coordinate, and fade to the pattern's mean as the footprint exceeds its period. Small emissive parts (reflector tips, dots) use the same idea: luminance scales with `min(1, size / footprint)`, so a part smaller than a pixel dims rather than sparkles.

`dev/backdrop.ts`: a `RenderLayer` named `sky` with a fullscreen sky triangle (`skyGrad`, `sunDisc`, `RenderOrder.SKY`) and a floor plane with `transparent: true`, `renderOrder = RenderOrder.TERRAIN`, `pristineGrid`, `fogK` and T03's alpha formula inside `abs(x) < 85`, 1 outside. It stands in for T03 and T04 only so that the mirror, bloom and silhouettes can be judged. It is not their look. It rendered correctly in the spike [O].

PYRAMID and PALM per the table in M5 to M7. **Mirror:** a second `Mesh` sharing the same geometry, with `MIRROR` and `BackSide`, `renderOrder = RenderOrder.MIRROR`. 12 meshes share geometry [O: 40 draws and 4 programs, no error].

**Visual iteration:** run A14, open the images, write what you saw into Progress. Expect to iterate on `uEdgeW` and `uBodyL`. Reject wash-out, edges that read white instead of palette hue, a reflection brighter than its source, or a reflection that detaches from the base. **Result:** the look exists on two objects. **Proof:** A13 to A16.

- **Additive draws depend on instance order, by rounding.** FIFO against rebuild at tier 3 read 38 to 41 bytes by 1, above A6's 32. With beams removed: 0 bytes. With beams alone: 86 and 105. - **A first fix was wrong.** Fixed row blocks by `chunk % span` made A6 pass and broke A7, 1077 bytes, because two rebuilds at distant chunks then placed rows differently. §8.1 requires a rebuild from equal rows to give one slot order, so only an order that does not depend on the absolute chunk works.
- **The reach-minus-one pixel control is empty.** It read about 100 bytes, which looked right, until sliding removed the beams' order effect and it read 0. By archetype it read 0 for PALM alone and for ARCOLOGY alone, and 10 bytes for all non-beam types together. Scale-in takes a row at the far edge to under a pixel. So the earlier figures measured slot order and nothing else. A2n is the real guard, as the plan said.
- **Post noise is larger than T00's one byte.** One frame repeated at tier 3 differed from itself by up to 43 bytes, by 1, with no state changed: A7 printed "the near frame repeated differs by up to 40" in a page where the pairs read 36, 4 and 6. The level is uneven and can hold across a page load. Tier 2 was 0 every time, so it comes with MSAA or with contention; I did not separate those. **Superseded by the V1 repair, next entries:** it is neither post nor contention.
- **The MSAA repeat noise is ANGLE's D3D11 path, triggered by blended draws** (V1 repair). One state drawn 25 times, full road scene, tier 4. On D3D11 ADD gave 16 to 17 distinct frames and MAX 14 to 16, though MAX is exact and order-free; replace-blending gave 1. At MSAA 0 on D3D11, and at MSAA 4 on ANGLE's Vulkan backend on the same RTX and page, every variant gave 1 of 25, and Vulkan gave 1 of 48 with no warm-up. D3D11 still gave 24 of 48 after a 300-frame warm-up, so it is not a one-time driver shader swap. The changing pixels sit inside the beams, scattered along their length (`scratchpad/T05/shots/diffmap-full-mapPng.png`). The beams only amplify a property of the platform: with them hidden the D3D11 noise fell to 1 to 5 bytes, and backdrop alone read 0 to 4. `--use-angle=gl` gave no context headless here (renderer null), so Vulkan is the control. Which part of D3D11 is responsible (ANGLE's translation, the driver, or the hardware's MSAA blending path) is not established.
